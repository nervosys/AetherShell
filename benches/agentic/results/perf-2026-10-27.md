## What this supports

- Module calls are faster in every round, 3.6x to 6.5x, median 4.56x (the
  follow-up measured 3.65x on Windows). Warm module requests (`math.abs`,
  `str.upper`) are about 2x faster in every round.
- `reduce` is faster in every round, 1.3x to 2.0x, median 1.91x (follow-up:
  2.27x).
- No other case moved consistently. Their rounds straddle 1, so no gain is
  claimed for them. Round 4 was slower for the final executable in six cases at
  once (0.37x to 0.64x), including cases its code does not touch. That points
  to the host, which shares its CPU with Windows, rather than to the code. It
  is also why the medians, not the means, are reported.
- The follow-up's neutral cases (string concat, sort, SQL, warm `1 + 1`) did
  not regress: their medians are 1.24, 1.27, 1.06 and 0.99, each within the
  spread of its rounds.

The gate for this rerun was "no regression, and the claimed gains reproduce";
both hold. Acceptance thresholds for later releases would need a quieter host
than this one.
